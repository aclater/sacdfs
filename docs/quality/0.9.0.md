# Quality of 0.9.0 against 0.8.0

Measured with: dsd-measure 0.1.2 (commit 3eaec2d) and [suites/sacdfs.toml](../../suites/sacdfs.toml);
sacdfs at commit 5efbc59f (release candidate; later commits change documentation, tests and file
times, not the converter's path; dsd2pcm 0.2.0 from crates.io, measured at its candidate commit e6ba8ba; the published crate's converter gives the same bytes: 400 conversions of the 40 quick-profile test signals, at every rate and cutoff and with both filters, compared byte for byte) and
0.8.0 at main commit 83baa60e (scarletbook-pcm 0.7.0), both built with Rust 1.100.0;
on an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395, on 2026-11-24. The two builds are measured in one run; the
suite's multitone signal has SHA-256
`100d1ba2cb46d98374ee5b8591b995712d12cd21c7d9370d50a1d68da3c030fc`. Changes beyond the suite's
measured run-to-run spread (any change of 0.01 dB or more; a null that is no longer identical)
are in bold.

The raw results of the run are in [0.9.0-results.csv](0.9.0-results.csv), and the null tests'
verdicts in [0.9.0-null.csv](0.9.0-null.csv) (one row per pair; `dsd-measure null --suite
suites/sacdfs.toml --source multitone.dsf --device "old ..." --device "new ..."` for each pair,
run with the 0.2.0 candidate at commit e6ba8ba; the published crate gives the same bytes, as stated above).

Cutoff 40 kHz and wide; gain 0 dB; 24-bit output.

"Null vs 0.8.0" is dsd-measure's null test of the 0.9.0 output against the 0.8.0 output on the
multitone signal: "identical" means the difference is zero to floating-point rounding, and a
number is the depth of the null, the difference's level relative to the signal.

`fast` is the filter of 0.8.0 and earlier, selected with `--pcm-filter fast`: all its figures equal
0.8.0's, and its output is the same bytes as dsd2pcm's `fast` filter (see the
[CHANGELOG](../../CHANGELOG.md)). From 0.9.0 the default is `precise`: it measures 19.9 to 29.0 dB
higher SINAD than 0.8.0 and 22.3 to 32.5 dB lower noise in the 20 Hz to 20 kHz band at every
rate and cutoff measured, and its measured worst alias image is 26.7 to 47.5 dB lower. Its SINAD
at 24 bits is within the limit of the output word length (the suite marks it "limited by its
24-bit output file").

At every rate and cutoff measured, `fast` measures exactly as 0.8.0 and the new
default `precise` measures higher SINAD, lower noise and better alias rejection than 0.8.0, with no metric worse.
